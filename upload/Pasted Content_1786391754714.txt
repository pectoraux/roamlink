# RoamLink — Build the Connectivity Commerce Business Engine

You are working directly inside the existing GitHub repository:

https://github.com/pectoraux/roamlink

RoamLink is an existing Next.js 16 + React 19 + TypeScript + PostgreSQL/Neon + Prisma + Bun eSIM reseller marketplace.

Your task is NOT to rebuild RoamLink from scratch.

Your task is to **inspect the existing codebase, preserve its good architecture, and transform it into a production-grade, commercially optimized connectivity commerce platform**.

The first product is travel eSIMs.

The long-term product is a broader connectivity marketplace capable of supporting multiple eSIM providers, regional products, top-ups, referrals, B2B customers, and eventually additional connectivity products.

# 2. PRESERVE THESE EXISTING ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLES

The existing repository has several principles that MUST remain.

## Provider abstraction

The application must remain provider-independent.

Business logic must never directly depend on Airalo.

Continue using:

ESIMProvider
PaymentProvider

and canonical provider-independent models.

Provider-native payloads must never leak into application/business logic.

Airalo must be an adapter, not the application architecture.

# 3. AIRALO IS THE INITIAL WHOLESALE SUPPLIER

RoamLink has an Airalo reseller relationship with these commercial terms:

* Program: Airalo for Resellers
* Payment: postpaid
* Credit limit: USD 10,000
* eSIM pricing: 20% commercial pricing/discount as shown in the agreement
* Monthly invoicing
* Invoice payable within 7 days
* Late interest: 1.5% per month or applicable legal maximum
* Taxes may apply
* Airalo can suspend additional purchases if undisputed invoices remain unpaid
* Credit-limit increases can be requested
* Airalo allows Partner to purchase, distribute and resell Services to End Users
* API integration is permitted where approved
* Airalo may change pricing
* The Partner is responsible for customer-facing refunds/replacements
* Top-up earnings may exist, but the current commercial terms show no top-up earnings rate, so DO NOT assume top-up revenue until explicitly configured

IMPORTANT:

Never hard-code the assumption that the Airalo "20%" value means a permanent 20% discount.

The platform must treat actual provider wholesale/net pricing returned by the provider as authoritative.

Store provider cost independently from customer selling price.

# 63. IMPORTANT BUSINESS RULE

Optimize for **contribution profit**, not revenue.

If Product A:

Revenue = $20
Contribution profit = $5

and Product B:

Revenue = $25
Contribution profit = $2

Product A is commercially better.

Analytics, recommendations and promotions should understand this distinction.

# 64. IMPORTANT BUSINESS RULE

Do not maximize margin at the expense of customer trust.

If two products are equivalent:

prefer the product with better economics.

But if one product is materially better for the customer:

recommend the better customer product.

Trust and repeat purchases are more valuable than squeezing an extra dollar from one transaction.

# 69. FINAL PRODUCT VISION

RoamLink is not merely:

"an eSIM store."

It is:

**A connectivity commerce platform that finds, prices, sells, provisions, manages and optimizes connectivity for travelers and organizations.**

The initial wedge is:

**travel eSIMs**

The initial geographic advantage is:

**Africa / emerging travel corridors**

The initial wholesale advantage is:

**Airalo postpaid reseller access with a $10,000 monthly credit limit**

The initial monetization is:

**eSIM margin + top-up margin**

The expansion is:

**regional products → repeat travel → referrals → affiliates → B2B → multi-provider routing → broader connectivity**

Build the software so that this business can grow from:

$10k monthly provider volume

to:

$50k

$100k

$250k

$500k

$1M+

without redesigning the architecture.

The most important principle is:

**Do not optimize RoamLink to sell the most eSIMs. Optimize RoamLink to generate the highest sustainable contribution profit while maintaining excellent customer experience and staying within provider credit, payment, regulatory and contractual constraints.**
