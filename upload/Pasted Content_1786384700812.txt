# 45. MOST IMPORTANT RULE

**RoamLink is not becoming two applications.**

It is becoming one connectivity platform with two major products:

```text
                    ROAMLINK
                       │
          ┌────────────┴────────────┐
          │                         │
        eSIM                 VIRTUAL NUMBER
          │                         │
       Data                    SMS / Voice
          │                         │
          └────────────┬────────────┘
                       │
              Unified RoamLink
                 infrastructure
```

Preserve the existing eSIM functionality.

Preserve the existing users.

Preserve the existing organizations.

Preserve the existing payment architecture.

Preserve the existing security model.

Preserve PostgreSQL.

Preserve the existing web/mobile architecture.

Extend the platform rather than forking it.

The goal is to make RoamLink a **unified global connectivity platform**, where customers can purchase and manage both mobile-data eSIMs and virtual communication numbers from the same account, same wallet/billing infrastructure, same organizations, same applications, and same backend.

### One strategic change I'd make beyond the prompt

I would **not launch virtual numbers globally immediately**. The architecture can be global, but the actual product catalog should initially be based on **countries where your chosen provider has reliable inventory and where the regulatory requirements are understood**.

That gives RoamLink a much stronger product model:

**eSIM → "Get connected when you travel."**
**Virtual Number → "Have a communication identity wherever you operate."**

And eventually the really powerful part is that one RoamLink account can contain both:

> 🇹🇬 **Togo eSIM** — 8.4 GB remaining
> 🇬🇭 **Ghana number** — +233… — SMS/Voice
> 🇺🇸 **US number** — +1… — SMS/Voice

That is a much more defensible product than simply adding another standalone virtual-number marketplace.
